# Prior tempering in the look-alike test

The prior over forms is raised to a power tau and renormalised over the look-alike candidate set: P_tau(form) = P(form)^tau / Z, Z = sum over candidates of P(c)^tau (hsr.lookalike.temper). Z is a finite sum with positive terms, so P_tau is a proper distribution on the candidate set for every tau >= 0. tau = 0 is the flat prior, tau = 1 the prior itself (renormalised over the set), tau > 1 sharpens it. Datasets, candidates and Laplace evidence are those of lookalike.md; no new fits.

P(true): mean posterior probability of the truth over the whole candidate set; log P: mean log P(true) over the datasets whose true-form fit succeeded (the others have P(true) = 0 for every tau); post. rank: median rank of the truth under the posterior (1 = MAP); mass: prior mass on the truth renormalised within A_5 (the candidates within 5 nats of the best evidence), mean over datasets with the truth in A_5; MAP: fraction of datasets where the truth is the posterior mode. The rank of the truth within A_5 under the prior (lookalike.md) does not depend on tau > 0, since P^tau is monotone in P.

Choice of tau: leave-one-truth-form-out over the grid 0, 0.05, ..., 2. For each true form, tau maximises the mean log P(true) on the datasets of all other true forms (datasets whose true-form fit failed are left out); the held-out score is the mean log P(true) on the left-out form's datasets at that tau. tau* (all): the maximiser on all datasets; tau* seen / novel: the maximiser on seen / novel truths only (in-sample).
