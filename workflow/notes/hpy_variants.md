## Summary

**Evaluation.** Every prior is now also scored by its own log P(tree) with no renormalisation ("raw").
All tree priors (fnprior, the old hsr.hpy, every variant here) define a proper distribution over the
same space, fnprior's coded trees, so raw scores compare them exactly. The honest proper baseline is
`sym0` (context-free symbol frequencies, a PY at depth 0 per child position): raw CV -15.1, 8 nats
below hpy2; drawing symbols uniformly ("uniform symbols") is improper (40% of its mass is on finite
trees). The PCFG grammars generate a different space (canonical sympy derivations, with literal
exponents and constant identities that the tree coding collapses), so raw tree-vs-PCFG differences are
approximate; term_root's raw CV is -13.9 (6.9 nats below hpy2). Two coding facts change how the old
renormalised table reads: 1569 of the 5309 support forms share their tree with another form (x^2,
x^c0, x^(1/3) are all pow(x, a)), so a tree prior's mass summed over the support is above one and the
renormalisation divided it among duplicates; and the "uniform over support" baseline is not a proper
prior over expressions (it beat fnprior/HPY on CellML and BioModels LOSO only because the support
contains the test forms).

**Properness.** Every variant's next-node distribution sums to one (to 1e-14) at every reachable
position, and P(finite tree) = 1 to six decimals at every fold, except `hpy2_dhpy` (the doubly
hierarchical source model) when predicting an unseen source: P(finite) = 0.959-0.969, i.e. 3-4% of
its mass is on infinite trees. fnprior: its conditionals sum to one over its own vocabulary, P(finite)
= 0.99997, but up to 0.19% of a second child's mass goes to y (a second variable). Mass on trees that
no form can have (non-canonical codes: argument orders sympy never produces, x*x for x^2, x - x, ...):
10% for fnprior and hpy2, 7-8% for depth-3/depth-conditioned HPY, 2% for the adaptors. The tree coding
reproduces fnprior's events exactly for all 5309 forms (n = 2 and 3), and hpy2 reproduces the old
hsr.hpy (CV raw -7.007 vs -7.009).

**What helped (raw CV, nats per form, vs hpy2 = -7.01; fnprior_kn -7.01):**

- Richer contexts (item 3): depth 3 (`hpy3`) +0.81; conditioning on node depth (`hpy2_depth`, item 4)
  +0.84; both (`hpy3_depth`) +1.15. Back-off order of a second child (drop sibling vs parent first,
  `hpy2_parent`) and graphical back-off with learned mixing (`hpy2_graph`, `hpy3_graph`) change
  nothing (within 0.03). Discounts/strengths are already per (depth, child position, component set):
  left and right children have separate groups (as in the old code), so no separate variant.
- Expression size (item 4): hpy2 and fnprior match the mean node count (9.5) but have too heavy a
  tail (90% quantile 21 vs 16 in the corpus) and too deep trees (mean depth 4.31 vs 4.13). Depth
  conditioning and depth 3 shorten the tail (q90 18-19); the adaptors match the corpus (q90 15-16,
  depth 4.1).
- Whole-form and subtree reuse (items 5 and 2): a PY adaptor over whole forms (`form_ad`, types vs
  tokens) +2.15; over every subtree (`tree_ad`, an adaptor grammar) +2.29; with a depth-3 base
  (`tree_ad3`) +2.32. Learned root discount a = 0.6, strength b = 1.5: strongly token-like. The gain
  is on forms whose tree occurs in training (94% of CV test forms): seen -3.15 vs -5.44. On novel
  forms the adaptors are better in CV (-31 vs -33) but 1-4 nats worse than the symbol-level models in
  the transfer and LOSO runs (e.g. LOSO CellML novel -21.6 vs -17.8), where they reserve less mass
  for new trees.
- Source-level hierarchy (item 1): no gain. `hpy2_src` (G_{s,u} ~ PY(G_u)) helps LOSO pooled for
  CellML (+1.8) and curvelib (+0.2) but hurts the others and every transfer (SABIO-RK -1.9), because
  the shared level then only sees table (type-like) counts, a flatter distribution than the pooled one;
  with equal weights it is 0.6 worse on average. The doubly hierarchical model (`hpy2_dhpy`) is worse
  still and improper for unseen sources (above). For a held-out source the prediction is the shared
  level, which is exactly the integral over a new, empty source-level restaurant.
- Source weighting: as before, equal weights beat pooled on average for every prior (e.g. tree_ad
  -7.08 vs -7.13, hpy3_depth -7.48 vs -7.64), with the exception of the source hierarchy.

- Combining: an adaptor over whole forms on the depth-3, depth-conditioned HPY (`form_ad3_depth`)
  is the best all-round prior: CV +2.25 (best `tree_ad3` +2.32), LOSO equal mean -6.99 (best of all;
  fnprior -7.87, hpy2 -7.89), and the best adaptor on novel forms. Subtree caching on top of form
  caching adds at most 0.07 in CV and nothing in LOSO.

**Recommendation.** For the DP base measure, `form_ad3_depth`: a PY adaptor over whole forms with a
depth-3, depth-conditioned HPY base, trained on all sources with equal weights. It is about 2.25 nats
per form better than fnprior in CV, 0.9 better in LOSO (equal weights), proper, and close to the
corpus size distribution. If the base measure should favour
new forms (e.g. the DP already handles reuse across datasets and the base is meant to spread mass over
novel structure), use `hpy3_depth`: +1.15 in CV, +0.4 in LOSO, best or near best on novel forms. Train
on all sources with equal weights.

**Correctness checks of the samplers** (tests/): predictive normalisation in every context for every
ContextSpec; seating invariants (customer and table counts, customers sent up = tables below) after
fits and after add/remove cycles; P(finite) from the branching fixed point equals the forward-sampling
frequency, and sampled tree frequencies equal the predictive; recovery of (d, theta) from a simulated
Pitman-Yor CRP; recovery of the root adaptor's discount and table count from simulated forms; and,
for the adaptor's Metropolis-Hastings sampler, the stationary distribution over seatings equals brute
force enumeration of every seating path on tiny data (an always-accept sampler fails this test), and
the posterior predictive with a live HPY base matches enumeration. MH acceptance on the real data:
0.999-1.000.

**Not done / caveats.** Full tree-substitution-grammar inference over arbitrary fragments (Cohn et al.
2010) was not attempted: the adaptor caches complete subtrees only (an adaptor grammar), which is the
simplest proper version. Predictives for held-out trees are the mixture over 10 retained samples of
each sample's product of conditionals (the model is not updated within a test tree), as is standard;
20 sweeps (10 burn-in). The source-level adaptor (per-source caches) was not run since the source
hierarchy did not help. Strength priors are Gamma(1, 1), which pulls weakly identified strengths down.
